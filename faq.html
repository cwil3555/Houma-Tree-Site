Good to know

Tree service questions, answered

The things Houma homeowners ask us most — on cost, permits, storms, and what to do when a tree comes down.

General

Do you charge for estimates?

No. Estimates are free and on-site, with no obligation to book.

What areas do you cover?

Houma and all of Terrebonne Parish, plus Thibodaux, Bayou Cane, Gray, Schriever, and Morgan City. If you’re nearby and not sure, just call and ask.

Do you haul away the wood?

Yes, haul-off is included unless you’d rather keep the wood. We can also cut the trunk to firewood length and leave it.

How soon can you come out?

For scheduled work, usually within a few days. Storm and emergency calls jump the line — though after a major hurricane the whole parish is calling at once.

Permits & rules in Terrebonne Parish

Do I need a permit to remove a tree in Terrebonne Parish?

For a tree on your own private property, a special parish permit generally isn’t required. The picture changes for trees in the public right-of-way (near the street or utility easements) and for wetland or drainage areas, which can involve parish or state rules. Because ordinances change and every lot is different, confirm with Terrebonne Parish before removal if there’s any doubt — we’re happy to flag anything that looks like it needs checking.

What about trees near power lines?

Never let anyone but a qualified crew work a tree touching or near a power line, and if a line is down, call the utility and 911 first. For trees growing into the lines themselves, the utility usually handles their own equipment — we handle everything on your side.

Do I need to call before stump grinding or digging?

Yes — call 811 before any digging or grinding so underground utilities get marked. It’s free, it’s the law, and it keeps a grinder from hitting a gas or fiber line. We confirm this is done before we grind.

Permit and rule information here is general guidance, not legal advice. Rules change — confirm specifics with Terrebonne Parish or the relevant agency before work begins.

Storms & emergencies

A tree just fell on my house. What do I do?

If anyone is hurt, or a power line is involved, call 911 and the utility first, and stay away from the tree. Then call us. Don’t try to cut a tree that’s under load — it can shift or snap back with enormous force.

Will my insurance cover storm tree removal?

Often, especially when a tree hits a covered structure like a roof or garage. We can’t speak for your policy, but we document emergency work with photos so you have what you need to file. Check your homeowner’s coverage.

Which trees fall most in storms around here?

Water oaks and pines are the usual culprits. Water oaks get brittle with age and shed big limbs; pines in newer subdivisions snap or uproot in high wind. Live oaks are sturdier but their sheer size makes any failure serious.
